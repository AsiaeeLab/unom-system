Undiagnosed No More

The Invisible Patient Finder

Find the patients the health record misses: open, tested tools for patients, clinicians, health systems, and developers.

  • Amir Asiaee Vanderbilt University Medical Center
  • Kaveh Aryan Department of Informatics, King's College London

undiagnosednomore.org

The problem

Some illnesses are real but invisible in the record

Long COVID, ME/CFS (chronic fatigue), fibromyalgia, and Lyme disease are hard to diagnose. Even after a clinician names the illness, the diagnosis code is often missing from the chart.

Without the code, the next clinician, post-COVID clinics, benefit programs, clinical trials, and studies that count patients by code can all miss the patient.

The diagnostic odyssey

In the roughly 100 days before their Long COVID was coded, patients had

What we found

Long COVID is coded for about 1 in 9 adults who ever had it

We compared how many adults say they have had Long COVID, in a national CDC survey, with how many carry its diagnosis code in their health record.

Less often for women

Women report Long COVID more often than men, but a smaller share of their Long COVID is coded: 0.79 times the share for men (95% interval 0.65 to 0.95). This comparison is not adjusted for health system.

Across the 10 health systems with enough patients, coding ranged from 0.79 to 2.37 times the expected rate (10th to 90th percentile).

Source: NIH All of Us Research Program, 23,790 adults with COVID-19 followed for 455 days, compared with the CDC Household Pulse Survey. Companion preprint on Long COVID coding (in preparation).

What we built

A summary-count model can leave the research enclave

All of Us data never leave its secure workspace, and neither may a model trained on individual records. So we fit our model only from privacy-protected summary counts, which the program allows us to export.

The result is a small, open file: a score over named, dated chart findings (new symptoms, tests, prescriptions, repeat visits) that another health system can run and check against our test patients.

0.64

AUROC at 18 health systems held out from fitting, for a model rebuilt from released counts, against 0.59 for a full-record model fitted to the same patients that cannot leave.

55 days

Median lead before the Long COVID code, among later-coded patients in the top 5% of scores (full-record model; analysis from this project).

AUROC is the chance the model ranks a patient who is later coded above one who is not: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.

Source: NIH All of Us Research Program, 5,760 patients at the 18 held-out health systems; AUROC 0.64, 95% interval 0.55 to 0.72, 0.05 above the full-record elastic net. On a random patient test (4,680 patients), the rebuilt model had an AUROC of 0.68. Companion manuscript on Long COVID coding (submitted).

Lived experience in the model

Training on patients' reports ranks uncoded patients higher

Most models learn only from patients who already have a code. We also trained on patients' own reports of a diagnosis, then asked which model ranks higher the people who report the illness but have no code for it.

Source: NIH All of Us Research Program, a random 20% validation split of each cohort (149,860 survey respondents for chronic fatigue, 150,340 for fibromyalgia, 97,590 women for endometriosis). Companion preprint on case-finding from patients' reports (in preparation).

Door 1 · Patients

The patient app: get ready for the visit

  • Validated questions: the NIH RECOVER symptom questions, the DePaul questions on crashes after activity (post-exertional malaise), condition self-checks, PHQ-9, and GAD-7, in English or Spanish.
  • A summary of what each answer means for a condition and what to discuss. Patients never see a probability.
  • A visit summary to print, or to download as a FHIR file that clinic software can read.
  • Answers stay on the device. No account, no tracking.

8 / 12

Door 2 · Clinicians

The clinician view: the evidence inside the chart

  • One card per condition, nine in all: Long COVID, ME/CFS, Lyme disease, lupus, fibromyalgia, migraine, endometriosis, depression, and anxiety.
  • Every card lists the dated chart findings behind it and the next step, such as a post-COVID clinic referral.
  • Built to open inside the EHR through SMART on FHIR, with no new login; tested in the public SMART sandbox.
  • Research decision support. It surfaces patterns for clinician review.

Door 4 · Developers

Open standards, and health data that stay local

Standards

  • FHIR R4 for reading the chart
  • SMART on FHIR to open inside the EHR
  • CDS Hooks to send a card when a chart opens
  • Standard codes: ICD-10-CM, LOINC, RxNorm, CPT

Where health data stay

  • The clinician view scores the chart in the browser.
  • The CDS Hooks service code keeps no copy of the chart data it receives and does not log it.
  • The patient app keeps answers on the device.
  • Every demo patient is synthetic.

All of Us disclosure rules

  • Only checked, aggregate numbers leave the research workspace.
  • Counts of 1 to 20 are suppressed, and other counts are rounded up to a multiple of 10.
  • The models were fit from those summary counts, so no individual record left.

Open model files, a reference scorer, and test patients let another health system check that its copy reproduces our scores.

What comes next

Roadmap and ask

  1. Works today

    Today

    Patient app, clinician view, Atlas, open models, and CDS Hooks, with synthetic patients. Launches from the public SMART sandbox; registered with Epic and athenaOne, with sandbox access pending.

  2. Next

    Pilot

    A silent-mode pilot at a health system (cards scored but not shown) to check them against real charts, and validation in a second national research network.

  3. Planned

    Marketplace

    Listings in EHR app marketplaces, and new conditions on the same engine, such as POTS and post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome.

Our ask: help us move from sandbox to a health system. A pilot site, EHR marketplace review, and patient advocates to review every new card.
