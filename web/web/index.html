What this site does

Undiagnosed No More finds patients whose invisible illness is missing from the health record, for nine conditions from Long COVID, ME/CFS, and Lyme disease to lupus, endometriosis, and depression. This tour follows the tools and the evidence behind them.

Data

In the NIH All of Us Research Program, only 24% of people in care for chronic fatigue have its code, against 85% for hypertension. All of Us records and surveys, with CDC, Census, and CMS data, show where nine invisible illnesses go uncoded.

AI models

Nine open models share one scorer. Each was fit outside the All of Us Workbench from released aggregate counts, and eight learn from patients' own reports of a diagnosis, so they find people whose diagnosis was never coded. The About page has the data, the models, and the four papers.

For clinicians

The clinician view opens inside the health record through SMART on FHIR. For each condition it shows the estimate, each dated finding with how much it moves it, the patient's answers, the criteria to check, and the next step.

For patients

Patients and advocates in the TOPx sessions described rebuilding their history for every clinician and wanting to approve what is shared. The app asks validated questions in English or Spanish, keeps answers on the device, and ends with a summary the patient prints or downloads for the visit.

For policymakers

The Atlas, for health systems and policy: pick a condition to see where and for whom it goes uncoded, what else is in the record, and what finding patients earlier costs. Its numbers are released aggregate estimates, not patient records.

For developers

The tools speak HL7 FHIR, SMART on FHIR, and CDS Hooks, and read USCDI data. A new condition is a new model file, not new software.
