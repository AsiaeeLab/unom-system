- Day 0: first U07.1 Condition dated on or after index_event.earliest_date (as in training) and on or before the scoring date; positive tests only if use_positive_tests.

The card says why.

Patient follow-up questions ([web/web/patient/instruments.js](../web/web/patient/instruments.js)):

- RECOVER 2024 index. 11 yes/no symptoms, points add up; 11 or more is the published "likely" range, given only when every symptom is answered. Scored only if the patient has had, or may have had, COVID-19. The app counts fatigue, brain fog, and chest pain without the index's severity scores and says so.
- DePaul Symptom Questionnaire: post-exertional malaise. 5 items, frequency and severity 0 to 4; positive if any item has both 2 or higher. Plus an unscored duration item.
- Post-COVID-19 Functional Status scale. One grade, 0 to 4. Skipped when the patient answers No to having had COVID-19.
- What changed since COVID. 5 unscored items for the clinician; the wording follows the COVID-19 answer.
- Long COVID: how long symptoms have lasted. Optional self-check (Part E): one question, whether symptoms have lasted 3 months or longer after COVID-19 (NASEM 2024 time criterion). Offered only after a Yes or Not sure to COVID-19.

Clinician checklist (shown, not scored; National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. A Long COVID Definition. 2024.):

- Symptoms lasting 3 months or longer after infection

Next steps on the card:

- Review symptoms against the NASEM 2024 Long COVID definition (symptoms lasting 3 months or longer after infection).
- Ask the patient to complete the RECOVER symptom questions and the DePaul post-exertional malaise screen.
- Consider referral to a post-COVID clinic or a relevant specialist.

- Other diagnoses do not exclude fibromyalgia, and fibromyalgia does not exclude other illnesses

Next steps on the card:

- Assess the patient with the 2016 revised fibromyalgia diagnostic criteria (Widespread Pain Index and Symptom Severity Scale).

- Three or more of: restlessness, tiring easily, trouble concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, poor sleep
- Clinically significant distress or impairment
- Not explained by a substance, another medical condition, or another mental disorder

Next steps on the card:

- Screen with the GAD-7 (the patient app has it); the USPSTF 2023 recommendation advises anxiety screening for adults under 65.
- If the GAD-7 is 10 or more, assess for an anxiety disorder against the DSM-5-TR criteria.

## Lupus: the chart rule in the criteria column

- Rule: [web/web/athena/rule.js](../web/web/athena/rule.js) (`evaluateLupusSignal`), shown in the lupus card's criteria column beside the model's estimate. Reads Condition and Observation (labs by LOINC and text), plus note text where available; a positive ANA at 1:80 or more is the entry criterion; within each domain only the highest-weighted criterion counts. Confirmed and possible (note-derived) evidence are kept apart. Criterion names match on word boundaries with the longest overlapping match kept, negated mentions ("no oral ulcers", "denies seizure") do not count, and white-cell and platelet counts are compared in 10^9/L whatever the unit (10*9/L, 10*3/uL, /uL).
- Missing data: the rule records which resources were readable. The lowest signal (1, "no chart-supported pattern") needs a negative ANA and readable Condition and Observation resources; otherwise the floor is 2, "limited evidence in this chart ... cannot rule out lupus". Missing tests (for example complement, anti-dsDNA, or urine protein) are listed on the card as data gaps.

## Missing data

Chart models (Long COVID, chronic fatigue, fibromyalgia, endometriosis, Lyme disease, lupus, migraine, depression, anxiety):

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| A code, test, procedure, or prescription that is not in the chart | Counted as absent (feature value 0). The models were fit the same way: a feature is the presence of a matching record in the window, with no imputation. |
| Too little history | Not scored, with the reason shown (rules under each condition). |
| Birth date missing or unusable | Not scored: "birth date missing or unusable (age is required)". The card says "No usable birth date on record". |
| Patient under 18 on day 0 (the COVID-19 index, or the scoring date) | Not scored: every model covers adults. Age is days / 365.25, as in the training cohorts. |
| Patient.gender other than female or missing | The female feature is 0, so the patient is scored like the reference group. |
| A FHIR search that fails, is forbidden, or is cut short (pagination limit, next link not followed, total above the entries returned) | The resource is unavailable, never empty: a model that reads it returns "could not score: <resource> unavailable" (status `could_not_score`), the card says "Could not score" and which records could not be read, and Needs review says that a result is missing. Patient, Condition, and Encounter are checked first; a resource read only by features is checked after eligibility, so an ineligible chart still says why. The CDS Hooks service returns one card for the conditions it could not score. Searches with records hidden by security labels count as readable. |
| Partial FHIR dates (YYYY or YYYY-MM) | Accepted. On a record: the first day they allow (January 1, or the first of the month). On Patient.birthDate: July 1, or the 15th (the training cohort used July 1 when only the year was known). A date field that is not a valid FHIR date is skipped; the scorer never stops on a chart's dates. |
| Records with no usable date | Count in no window and cannot be the COVID-19 index; an undated target diagnosis (for example an undated U09.9) counts as already recorded, so the card is not scored. |
| Records dated after the scoring date | Ignored by every rule and feature (a later Long COVID code does not hide an earlier score). |
| Refuted or entered-in-error records | Ignored: Conditions whose verificationStatus is refuted or entered-in-error, and any record whose status is entered-in-error. |
| Features whose training counts were suppressed (1 to 20) | Left out of the model; only features with every released cell above 20 were eligible. |

## Patient questionnaire

One questionnaire for everyone, saved as a FHIR QuestionnaireResponse for `https://undiagnosednomore.org/fhir/Questionnaire/unm-patient-symptom-check` version 1.3.0; item wording and sources in [submission/app_questionnaire.md](../submission/app_questionnaire.md). The patient reviews and corrects the answers before sharing. The demo flags a chart for review if the band is High, or RECOVER is 11 or more, or the post-exertional malaise screen is positive; this combined rule is exploratory (not evaluated for how many patients it flags or misses) and is labeled so in the app. In the clinician view, one answer per condition updates the chart estimate (next section). Supported versions for import: 1.0.0, 1.1.0, 1.2.0, 1.3.0.

| Part | Scoring and when it is shown |
|---|---|
| RECOVER 2024 index | 11 yes/no symptoms, points add up; 11 or more is the published "likely" range, given only when every symptom is answered. Scored only if the patient has had, or may have had, COVID-19. The app counts fatigue, brain fog, and chest pain without the index's severity scores and says so. |
| DePaul Symptom Questionnaire: post-exertional malaise | 5 items, frequency and severity 0 to 4; positive if any item has both 2 or higher. Plus an unscored duration item. |
| Post-COVID-19 Functional Status scale | One grade, 0 to 4. Skipped when the patient answers No to having had COVID-19. |
| What changed since COVID | 5 unscored items for the clinician; the wording follows the COVID-19 answer. |
| Long COVID: how long symptoms have lasted | Optional self-check (Part E): one question, whether symptoms have lasted 3 months or longer after COVID-19 (NASEM 2024 time criterion). Offered only after a Yes or Not sure to COVID-19. |
| ME/CFS: the core symptoms | Optional self-check (Part E): 4 yes/no/not sure questions for the IOM 2015 core symptoms; post-exertional malaise comes from the DePaul screen. Result: matches, does not match, or not finished. |
| Fibromyalgia: where it hurts and how you feel | Optional self-check (Part E): pain in 19 areas in the past 7 days (Widespread Pain Index), 3 severity items and 3 past-6-month items (Symptom Severity Scale), and a 3-month duration item. Result follows the 2016 rule (WPI 7+ and SSS 5+, or WPI 4 to 6 and SSS 9+; pain in 4 of 5 regions; 3 months), with WPI and SSS exported. |
| Lyme disease: possible exposure | Optional (Part E): 3 exposure questions (tick attached, time outdoors where Lyme disease is common, expanding rash), written for the app from CDC guidance. Not scored. |
| Depression: the PHQ-9 | Optional (Part E): the published PHQ-9, 9 items over the last 2 weeks, each 0 to 3, plus its unscored difficulty question. Total 0 to 27 and the published severity (0 to 4 none-minimal, 5 to 9 mild, 10 to 14 moderate, 15 to 19 moderately severe, 20 to 27 severe), given only when all 9 items are answered. Item 9 (thoughts of being better off dead or of self-harm) answered other than Not at all shows the patient the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline at once and puts a notice at the top of From the patient. Shown, not combined with any chart estimate. |
| Anxiety: the GAD-7 | Optional (Part E): the published GAD-7, 7 items over the last 2 weeks, each 0 to 3. Total 0 to 21 with the published cutpoints 5, 10, and 15 for mild, moderate, and severe anxiety, given only when all 7 items are answered. Shown, not combined with any chart estimate. |

## Combining the chart and the answers

When an imported QuestionnaireResponse has a result for a condition's instrument, the clinician view multiplies the chart estimate's odds by that result's published likelihood ratio (Bayes' rule) and shows the combined probability, times the average, band, and next steps, with the chart-only values beside them and the answer as a finding in the card's Why list. This treats the answers and the chart findings as independent given the condition, the same assumption the chart models make for their findings; where the chart already codes the same symptoms the answers count, the update overstates. Each ratio was measured against a reference close to the chart model's target, and none in the app's own population. One instrument per condition, because the instruments overlap. Not combined, because no published ratio against a comparable reference exists: the DePaul post-exertional malaise screen alone, the National Academies time question, the Post-COVID-19 Functional Status scale, the Lyme exposure questions, and the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 (their published accuracy is against interview diagnoses, not against the depression and anxiety models' target, a self-reported diagnosis that was never coded).

| Condition | Answer used | Likelihood ratio | Measured as | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long COVID | RECOVER 2024 index | 2.83 if 11 or more points; 0.46 if under 11 points | the index at 11 or more points against a Long COVID code (U09.9) in the linked records of 1,501 RECOVER-Adult participants | [Castro et al., JAMIA 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag115) |
| Chronic fatigue (ME/CFS) | ME/CFS self-check (IOM 2015 core symptoms) | 1.62 if matches the criteria pattern; 0.47 if does not match | questionnaire-based IOM 2015 criteria against a physician panel's chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis, among community adults with chronic fatigue | [Jason et al., Diagnostics 2015](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics5020272) |
| Fibromyalgia | Fibromyalgia self-check (2016 criteria) | 8.46 if matches the criteria pattern; 0.7 if does not match | the patient-completed 2016 criteria against a patient-reported physician diagnosis of fibromyalgia, among 3,276 primary care patients | [Srinivasan et al., ACR Open Rheumatology 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr2.1006) |
